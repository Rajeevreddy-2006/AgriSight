# Integrated Pest Management principles (EXAMPLE PLACEHOLDER)

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines several methods so that chemicals are used
only when needed. It lowers cost, protects natural enemies of pests and slows the
development of resistance.

1. Prevention: healthy seed, resistant or tolerant varieties, crop rotation, field sanitation and balanced fertiliser.
2. Monitoring: walk the field regularly, look at both sides of leaves, and note when a problem starts and how fast it spreads.
3. Action thresholds: act when the pest or disease level is high enough to cause economic loss, as advised by local experts.
4. Non-chemical control first: remove infected plant parts, use traps, encourage natural enemies, and use biological products where recommended.
5. Chemical control as the last step: use only recommended products at label doses, and rotate products with different modes of action.
